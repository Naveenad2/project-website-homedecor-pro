Ordinary glass breaks into long, sharp shards. That's why it shouldn't be used where people could walk into it, fall against it or stand underneath it. For those places you need **safety glass** — and there are two kinds, toughened and laminated, which protect you in completely different ways.

## Toughened glass: strength

Toughened (or tempered) glass is ordinary glass heated to over 600 °C and then cooled rapidly with jets of air. The surfaces cool and set first, which locks them in compression while the core stays in tension. The result is roughly **four to five times stronger** than ordinary glass of the same thickness.

When toughened glass does break, the stored energy shatters the whole pane into small, blunt granules instead of dangerous shards.

Two things to know:

- **It can't be cut, drilled or edged after toughening.** Every piece is made to exact size, with holes and notches planned in advance.
- **The whole pane breaks at once**, leaving an open gap where the glass was.

## Laminated glass: it holds together

Laminated glass is two or more panes bonded with a tough plastic interlayer, usually PVB. If it's struck, the glass may crack but the pieces stay stuck to the interlayer, and the panel stays in its frame. That's why laminated glass is the standard for overhead glazing and a strong choice for security.

The interlayer has two bonuses: it **reduces noise** — acoustic grades especially — and it **blocks most UV light**, which protects floors, curtains and furniture from fading.

## Side by side

| | Toughened | Laminated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it protects you | Much stronger; breaks into blunt granules | Holds together when broken; stays in the frame |
| Strength | About 4–5× ordinary glass | Similar to ordinary glass, unless its panes are toughened |
| After breaking | The opening is left clear | The panel stays in place |
| Noise reduction | Like ordinary glass of the same thickness | Better — especially acoustic laminate |
| UV protection | Minimal | Blocks most UV |
| Cut on site? | Never | Ordinary laminated, yes; toughened-laminated, no |
| Typical uses | Doors, shower screens, partitions, tabletops | Skylights, railings, shopfronts, noisy bedrooms |

## Toughened-laminated: both at once

For the most demanding places, two toughened panes are laminated together. You get the strength of toughened glass and the post-breakage safety of laminated glass: if one pane breaks, the other pane and the interlayer keep the panel in place. This is our usual recommendation for frameless [glass railings](/products/glass-handrails) and for [skylights](/products/laminated-glass/glass-skylights).

| Where | Recommended glass |
| --- | --- |
| Frameless glass doors | Toughened |
| Shower partitions | Toughened, commonly 8–10 mm |
| Office & home partitions | Toughened — acoustic laminated where privacy matters |
| Staircase & balcony railings | Toughened-laminated |

## How to tell if glass is toughened

- **Look for a mark.** Toughened glass usually carries a permanent stamp or etched mark in one corner.
- **Use polarised sunglasses.** Looking through them, you'll often see faint patterns of dark spots or stripes on toughened glass.
- **Check the edges.** Toughened glass is always edged before toughening, so its edges are finished, never raw-cut.
- **When in doubt, ask.** A good supplier will confirm the glass specification in writing.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. Using ordinary glass in doors or low-level panels where people could fall against it.
2. Using single toughened glass overhead — if it breaks, it falls. Overhead glass should be laminated.
3. Ordering glass before the site is finished — toughened glass can't be trimmed later.
4. Choosing glass by thickness alone — the type of glass matters more than a few millimetres.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which is safer, toughened or laminated glass?

They are safe in different ways. Toughened glass is stronger and breaks into blunt pieces; laminated glass holds together when broken. Where glass is overhead or guards a drop, toughened-laminated glass gives you both.

### Is laminated glass bulletproof?

No. Standard laminated glass resists break-ins far better than ordinary glass, but bullet-resistant glass is a specialist multi-layer product.

### Can toughened glass break on its own?

Rarely, toughened glass can break without an obvious impact — for example from edge damage during handling or a tiny inclusion in the glass. Quality glass and careful installation make this very unlikely, and if it happens the glass breaks into blunt granules.

### What glass should I use for a balcony railing?

Toughened-laminated glass, so the panel stays in place even if one layer breaks. The thickness depends on the height and fixing system — common combinations are 6+6 mm or 8+8 mm. See our [balcony glass railings](/products/glass-handrails/balcony-glass-railing).
