It's the first question almost every homeowner asks us: *uPVC or aluminium?* Both are good materials — they're simply good at different things. Here's how they compare in Kerala's conditions, and how to decide room by room.

## The short answer

Choose **uPVC** for most windows in a Kerala home: it insulates better, can't rust or corrode in humid or salty air, and comes in warm wood-grain finishes.

Choose **system aluminium** for very large sliding doors, corner windows and minimal modern elevations, where you want the slimmest frames and the biggest panes of glass.

Many of the best homes we work on use **both**.

## Heat: the frame matters, the glass matters more

Aluminium is an excellent conductor of heat; uPVC is a poor one. On a sun-baked west wall, an ordinary aluminium frame gets hot and passes heat into the room, while a uPVC frame stays closer to room temperature.

But glass makes up most of a window's area, so for heat the **glass choice matters even more than the frame**. On sunny walls and in air-conditioned rooms, ask about solar-control glass or [double-glazed windows](/products/saint-gobain-upvc-windows/double-glazed-windows) — whichever frame you choose.

## Rain and humidity

Kerala's monsoon tests every joint. Both materials can be completely weather-tight when the system is well engineered and properly installed: gaskets, drainage and how the frame is sealed to the wall matter more than the material itself. (If you're fighting leaks today, read [why windows leak in the monsoon](/blog/stop-window-leaks-monsoon-kerala).)

Where the two differ is humidity and salt. uPVC can't rust or corrode at all. Aluminium relies on its powder-coated or anodised finish and on quality fittings — perfectly fine when the quality is good, but worth checking carefully if you live near the sea.

## Strength, size and the look you want

This is aluminium's home ground. Its strength lets slim frames carry very large, heavy glass. Dimore's Vistaslim system, for example, takes sashes up to 3000 mm high and 1800 mm wide on heavy-duty rollers. If you want a floor-to-ceiling sliding wall onto a garden, or a corner window with almost no frame, choose system aluminium.

uPVC frames are wider, but steel reinforcement lets them handle generous windows and doors — and wood-grain uPVC gives a warmth that aluminium can't match.

## Using both in one home

There's no rule that a house must use one material. A combination we often recommend: uPVC windows in the bedrooms and kitchen for insulation and quiet, and a system aluminium sliding door in the living room for the widest possible view. Choosing complementary colours keeps the elevation consistent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which lasts longer, uPVC or aluminium windows?

Both last for many years when they are good quality and properly installed. uPVC cannot corrode; the life of aluminium depends on the quality of its finish and hardware, especially near the coast.

### Are aluminium windows hotter than uPVC windows?

Ordinary aluminium frames conduct more heat than uPVC. The glass has an even bigger effect, so pair either frame with solar-control or double glazing on sunny walls.

### Can uPVC be used for large sliding doors?

Yes — uPVC sliding and French doors work well for most doorways. For very large, heavy panels or the slimmest frames, system aluminium is the better choice. See our [aluminium sliding doors](/products/system-aluminium-windows/aluminium-sliding-doors).

### Which is better near the sea, uPVC or aluminium?

uPVC is naturally immune to salt corrosion. Aluminium also works near the coast when it has a good powder-coated or anodised finish and quality fittings, and is rinsed regularly.
